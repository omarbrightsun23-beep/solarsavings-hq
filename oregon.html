💡 Homeowner Knowledge Base

Oregon Solar & Financial FAQ

Under Section 25D of the U.S. Internal Revenue Code (Residential Clean Energy Credit), homeowners claim a direct 30% dollar-for-dollar reduction against federal income tax liability on all solar equipment, inverters, racking, labor, and battery storage. If your credit exceeds tax owed in Year 1, the remaining balance rolls over indefinitely to future tax years via IRS Form 5695.
Oregon enforces 1:1 Net Metering + Energy Trust of Oregon Rebates. When your solar panels generate more kilowatt-hours than your home consumes during peak daylight, surplus energy flows back into the grid, earning credits against nighttime utility usage.
With an average utility rate of $0.13/kWh and 4.2 daily peak sun hours, residential solar payback in Oregon is typically 8.0 - 9.7 years. Homes with higher electric bills or adding battery storage under Time-of-Use rates achieve payback toward the faster end of this range.
According to research by the Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory (LBNL), owned solar panels increase home market appraisal value by approximately $4.00 per installed Watt. Crucially, most U.S. states have statutory 100% property tax exemptions for residential renewable energy systems, ensuring your assessed property tax does not increase.
